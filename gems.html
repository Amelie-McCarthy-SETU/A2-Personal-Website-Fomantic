Welcome to my gemstone collection!

Here is where I document my current collection of precious gems and minerals, their chemical composition, and some notes on my findings! Currently I have over 100 samples in my collection, found near me, bought online or collected from rock shops. I also have a form below for telling me which of these gems is your favourite, and why!
Please note I am not currently sell items from my collection.


My Current Collection...



Amethyst
SiO2
My favourite gem of a purple hue, of which I have over 20 samples.


Emerald
Be3Al2(SiO3)6
I have one sample currently of this uncommon gem. It's use is mainly for jewellery and is expensive and hard to find.


Diamond
C
The most expensive gemstone, which can be found naturally or produced in a lab at a lower cost. Made of pure carbon and extremely rare. I only own 1 rough piece.


Sapphire
Al203
I have 4 samples of this bright blue mineral. It can be used in jewellery, and varies in colour from blue to red (rubies)!


Opal
SiO2 · nH2O
A very unique gem, known for its reflection of light into a trippy rainbow appearance. I own 2 samples, a natural piece and an artificial piece.


Quartz
SiO2
The most common and inexpensive gem, and can be found all over the world. I have many samples from near me and abroad. Can come in many colours.


Fluorite (purple)
CaF2
Comes in a range of colours, from purple to green based on chemical composition. I have 5 natural samples of the purple variant from France. These fade easily in sunlight.


Fluorite (green)
CaF2
Comes in a range of colours, from purple to green based on chemical composition. The most common fluorite samples I own are green.

A gemstone (also called a fine gem, jewel, precious stone, semiprecious stone, or simply gem) is a piece of mineral crystal which, when cut or polished, is used to make jewelry or other adornments. Certain rocks (such as lapis lazuli, opal, and obsidian) and occasionally organic materials that are not minerals (such as amber, jet, and pearl) may also be used for jewelry and are therefore often considered to be gemstones as well. Most gemstones are hard, but some softer minerals such as brazilianite may be used in jewelry because of their color or luster or other physical properties that have aesthetic value. However, generally speaking, soft minerals are not typically used as gemstones by virtue of their brittleness and lack of durability.

Found all over the world, the industry of coloured gemstones (i.e. anything other than diamonds) is currently estimated at US$1.55 billion as of 2023 and is projected to steadily increase to a value of $4.46 billion by 2033. A gem expert is a gemologist, a gem maker is called a lapidarist or gemcutter; a diamond cutter is called a diamantaire.

Source: Gemstone - Wikipedia

Gemstones are used for jewellery, decoration, spiritual reasons or in tools (such as diamond bit tools).
